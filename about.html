Who I am

I’m Jacob, a product manager and project manager in Auckland. I’ve been into digital since my first Amstrad, and along the way I’ve been a designer, a tutor, a network tech, a producer and a marketer. That’s why I’m comfortable in most of the places a product passes through.

I wear many hats and own the whole lifecycle, from concept and customer discovery sessions, to prototyping and scoping the work needed. I can take that work to developers or deliver it myself with AI, run the beta release programme, and then deliver it to market.

I am a nerd, I watch YouTube videos on AI for fun. Passions are varied and run deep. I have a collection of vintage Apple computers (some still in daily use). My homelab and home automation setups are constant inspiration for more tasks and projects to do. Big fan of LEGO with about 67,356 pieces in my collection, sci-fi nerd with a love of Neal Stephenson, The Expanse, Star Trek and BattleTech. I build my dream cities every day in Minecraft and Cities: Skylines. Currently enjoying Letterkenny.

How I work

Naturally, wearing many hats means I tend to get involved in the difficult problems. Scenarios where different stakeholders want different things, there's pressure to deliver quality but also needed fast and cheap, and the backlog keeps growing. That is where I am in my element, working with people to unpick what the priorities actually are, balancing the objectives, eliminating the ambiguity.

One thing I think I'm genuinely good at is working across different team sizes and structures. I've done PRINCE2 Agile training, I happily work in Agile or waterfall. I'll pick what parts of frameworks work best for the scenario - I'm method agnostic. I've worked with teams as small as a single developer, and as large as multi-squad programmes working towards one shared goal. The way I work changes depending on the context - what doesn't change is that I give developers the full picture. I tell them what we're building and why, and trust them with how we're going to do that.

I'll use whatever gets the job done, and if the right tool doesn't exist, I'll build it. I recently took a 4-hour weekly reporting process and automated it to run every day in the background.
